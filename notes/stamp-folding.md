# Stamp folding

## Status

- n = 1 to 13: lower bounds certified by listing every folding (exact counts). The lists are generated by depth-first search, building the stack from the bottom and matching each edge's creases like brackets, and match OEIS A000136.
- n = 1 to 3: upper bounds proved in Lean from the trivial bound n! (`StampFoldingSmall.lean`).
- n = 4 to 7: upper bounds proved in Lean (`StampFoldingCount.lean`). A folding maps injectively to the list of its stamps' levels, a permutation whose creases on each edge don't cross. The kernel lists all permutations, by inserting each element at every position, and counts those that pass. n = 4 to 7 take about 10 seconds together. n = 8 (40320 permutations) didn't finish in 10 minutes.
- n = 14 and up: a full list is too large for the 500 kB limit (n = 13 is 288 kB with xz, and the counts grow by about 3.5 per stamp). Only partial lists, giving weaker lower bounds, fit. These are in `certificates/blocked.yml`.

## Ideas

- Upper bounds for n = 8 and beyond: replace the list of all permutations with a pruned search that places stamps one at a time and abandons a branch as soon as two creases cross, as in `SchurSmallUpper.lean`. Prove it complete for the foldings, rather than for all permutations. The counts grow by about 3.5 per stamp, so even a pruned search has to visit every folding, which limits it to n of about 12 to 14.
- Lower bounds for n ≥ 14 need a Lean proof as well: the same verified count, proved to be exact. A lower bound means showing that every list it counts is a folding, which is the converse of `ok_levels`.
