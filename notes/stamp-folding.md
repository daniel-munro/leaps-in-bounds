# Stamp folding

## Status

- n = 1 to 13: lower bounds certified by listing every folding (exact counts). The lists are generated by depth-first search, building the stack from the bottom and matching each edge's creases like brackets, and match OEIS A000136.
- n = 1 to 3: upper bounds proved in Lean from the trivial bound n! (`StampFoldingSmall.lean`).
- n = 4 to 7: upper bounds proved in Lean (`StampFoldingCount.lean`). A folding maps injectively to the list of its stamps' levels, a permutation whose creases on each edge don't cross. The kernel lists all permutations, by inserting each element at every position, and counts those that pass. n = 4 to 7 take about 10 seconds together. n = 8 (40320 permutations) didn't finish in 10 minutes.
- n = 14 and 15 (November 2026): lower bounds certified by generators (`format: generator`, output `stacks`) that print every folding, written by an AI agent (Claude Opus 5.5). The search inserts the stamps into the stack one at a time, in order; inserting stamp k adds the crease (k - 1, k), and the gaps where k can go are those in the same region of the nested creases on that edge as stamp k - 1, found by one scan of the stack. A crossing among placed creases can't be undone by later insertions, so this finds every folding exactly once. The same generator, for maps, is in `certificates/map-folding/`. n = 14 prints 23 MB in about 2 s, and generating and checking it takes under a minute; n = 15 prints 83 MB in about 6 s, and generating and checking it takes 3 to 4 minutes on a heavily loaded machine, with 1.4 GB of memory.
- n = 16 and up: the full list (n = 16 is 17,622,410 foldings, 300 MB) is over the 200 MB the folding checker reads at once. Listed in `certificates/blocked.yml`.

## Ideas

- A streaming folding checker (reading generator output line by line, keeping only a hash of each stack for the distinctness check, or requiring the stacks in increasing order so that distinctness is a comparison with the previous one) would lift the 200 MB limit. n = 16 (300 MB) would then take maybe 15 minutes to check, n = 17 (59,231,690 foldings, 1.07 GB) maybe an hour, at the current checker's speed of about 30 to 40 µs per stack. The generator prints the stacks in search order, not sorted; a sorted order would need a different search (building the stack from the bottom, as the n ≤ 13 lists were).

- Upper bounds for n = 8 and beyond: replace the list of all permutations with a pruned search that places stamps one at a time and abandons a branch as soon as two creases cross, as in `SchurSmallUpper.lean`. Prove it complete for the foldings, rather than for all permutations. The counts grow by about 3.5 per stamp, so even a pruned search has to visit every folding, which limits it to n of about 12 to 14.
- Lower bounds for n ≥ 14 need a Lean proof as well: the same verified count, proved to be exact. A lower bound means showing that every list it counts is a folding, which is the converse of `ok_levels`.
