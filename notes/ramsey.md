# Ramsey numbers

## Checker speed is the main constraint

The checker (`ramsey_check_witness` in `scripts/certificates.py`) is a pure-Python branch and bound (MCQ colouring bound). It detects circulant graphs (i -> i + 1 is an automorphism) and then only searches cliques through vertex 0, which makes circulants far faster to check than other graphs of the same size. Rough cost: the work is about the total size of the candidate sets the search visits, and the checker runs about 2.5e5 to 4e5 units per second, so a 2e7-unit witness takes about a minute. Proving that there is no clique of size ω + 1 is the expensive part, and it grows quickly with n and ω, so the largest checkable witnesses for R(9,s) and R(10,s) are around 550 to 700 vertices.

Non-circulant witnesses are much slower: checking that the disjoint union of two copies of the 204-vertex R(7,7) witness has no independent set of size 13 hadn't finished after several minutes. So disjoint unions and joins of different graphs are a poor route to verified bounds, even though they're the classical constructive bounds. Lexicographic products of circulants are circulant: for G on Z_n and H on Z_m, G[H] is the circulant on Z_nm with x ~ y when x - y mod n is in G's connection set, or x - y = n·k with k mod m in H's connection set. Its clique number is ω(G)ω(H) and its independence number α(G)α(H).

## Initial verified lower bounds (Nov 2026)

A bounded search, by an AI agent (Claude), gave the first verified lower bounds for the 49 R(r,s) with 4 ≤ r ≤ 10 and s ≤ 15 that had none, other than R(8,12), which has an attested 523 that a new certificate must match. Every witness is a circulant, checked in at most about a minute. Candidates were:

- cyclotomic circulants on Z_p, p prime up to about 940: x ~ y when x - y is in a union of cosets of the subgroup of k-th powers, k ≤ 10 (Paley graphs for k = 2), closed under negation;
- circulants on Z_n, 40 ≤ n < 700, whose connection set is a union of orbits of the multiplicative group generated by some unit m and -1, with 3 to 14 orbits (all unions when few, else 40 to 60 random ones per group);
- the verified circulant witnesses already here, E_k and K_k, complements of all of these, and lexicographic products of two of them.

Clique and independence numbers were computed in C, with a cap on the search so that graphs too slow to check were dropped, and each target got the largest graph whose check (a C imitation of the Python checker) stayed under about 1.8e7 work units.

Highlights: R(4,12) >= 128 (cubes mod 127) and R(9,9) >= 563 (a circulant on Z_562 whose connection set is a union of orbits under multiplication by 5 and -1, with clique and independence number 8) are at or near the literature values (128 and 565). The rest are well below the literature, typically by 10 to 40%, and further for R(8,13), R(10,10) and R(10,15). Several targets reuse one graph (for example, Z_562 for R(9,9) to R(10,11)).

## Ideas not yet tried

- Bigger witnesses are limited by the checker, not the search: checking even the Z_641 cyclotomic graph with ω = 9 and α = 14 for R(10,15) needs about 1.8e8 work, around 10 minutes, and the literature witnesses for r, s >= 8 have 300 to 1300 vertices. A larger time budget, or attestations, would allow much stronger circulants.
- Tabu or simulated-annealing search over circulant connection sets (as in the literature for R(5,15) and others), seeded from the graphs here, should improve most of these, as long as the results stay small enough to check.
- Many literature bounds come from non-circulant constructions (such as Xu-Xie-Radziszowski's R(r, s+t-1) constructions, or 2-cyclic and block-circulant graphs), which this checker handles slowly. A checker that exploits other symmetry (for example, a declared automorphism group) would open those up.
